Mathias Dépfner, chief executive of Axel Springer, said that many people had “intentionally misunderstood” Vance’s address at last weekend's Munich Security Conference, in which the US vice-president stunned the audience by claiming that restrictions on freedom of expression in Europe were a greater threat to the region than Russia or China. Vance also called for an end to the political isolation of the far right, just over a week before German federal elections in which the Alternative for Germany (AfD) party is expected to secure a historic second-place finish. The speech drew a sharp response from German politicians, including Chancellor Olaf Scholz and opposition leader Friedrich Merz. Dopfner — a German national who co-owns the Berlin-based media empire — said Vance’s intended message was that Washington wanted to work with Europe but that the continent needed first to define what it stood for and stop being afraid of its people. “Honestly, I think it’s an inspiring message,’ he said. “You don't have to take everything literally, but you should try to take it seriously. “And that most of the Europeans just reacted . . . ina kind of whiny tone — | think that is unsmart, it’s unstrategic, and it’s even dangerous because we need a transatlantic security alliance and we need a transatlantic trade relationship.” Donald Trump has threatened to impose tariffs on EU goods and to withdraw security guarantees for the continent. Dopfner, a self-described contrarian who has railed against the “enslavement” of so-called woke culture, also played down the seriousness of Trump’s recent attack on Politico as a “misunderstanding”.
