TECH TRADER Intel shouldn't discuss selling itself for parts while trying to find a chief executive-especially when a deal may not even be possible. Intel’s Future Sull Looks Murky. Don’t Chase the Stock. ntel shares have surged in recent weeks on President Donald Trump’s promise to support domestic chip manu- facturing and reports that semiconductor rivals are weighing options to purchase parts of the company. Upon closer examination of the de- velopments, investors should be cau- tious. The likelihood of any transfor- mative deals in the near term is low, and not much has changed regarding Intel’s challenges. It may be wise for investors not to chase the stock. The rally started earlier this month after Vice President JD Vance vowed that more chips would be made in the US. during his speech at an artificial- intelligence conference in France. Intel stock gained further steam on reports from Bloomberg and The Wall Street Journal that Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing was considering tak- ing a stake in Intel’s chip factories, while Broadcom was looking at buy- ing Intel’s product business. All told, Intel stock rose by nearly 40% over five trading sessions through Tuesday. But the flurry of news reports at times contradicted one another, with one saying that the Trump administra- tion raised the idea of TSMC taking a controlling stake in Intel’s chip facto- ries, and another citing a White House official saying that Trump probably wouldn’t support such a deal. Allowing TSMC to gain control of Intel’s factories would be an odd deci- sion, as it would grant the Taiwanese company a de facto monopoly in global advanced chip manufacturing.
