CHESS LEONARD BARDEN Vincent Keymer achieved a career-best result last weekend when the 20- year-old German defeated world No 1 Magnus Carlsen in the semi-final of the Weissenhaus Grand Slam. He went on to outplay the world No 2 Fabiano Caruana in the two-day final, with winning margins of 1.5-0.5 in both matches. He called it “the greatest success of my career’. First prize was $200,000, while Carlsen earned $100,000 by defeating Uzbekistan’s Javokhir Sindarov 2-0 in the third-place match. Keymer already had a near miss against Carlsen in the 2023 World Cup, where he won their first game before losing 3.5-2.5 after tie-breaks. As a child, Keymer was a piano prodigy, but unlike former musical greats Francois-André Philidor and Mark Taimanov, he found it too difficult to combine chess and concerts.
